Coordination of Benefits: Who Pays First?

Learn how to decide primary, secondary, and tertiary insurance with colorful rules, case examples, mini-games, and concept-checking questions.

First: What is Coordination of Benefits?

Coordination of Benefits, or COB, is the system used when a patient has more than one insurance plan. The biller must decide which plan pays first, second, and sometimes third.

COB answers one question: Which insurance gets the first bill?
Primary = pays first Secondary = pays after Tertiary = pays third Medicaid = usually last

Do Not Guess

Students often guess by choosing the plan with the best benefits or lowest copay. That is not the rule.

The plan with the lowest copay is not automatically primary.
Use the COB rules in order.

The Insurance Line-Up

Subscriber PlanThe patient owns this plan. Usually pays before a dependent plan.
Active EmploymentCurrent job coverage usually pays before retiree or COBRA coverage.
Injury SourceWork injury may go to workers’ compensation before regular insurance.
Last ResortMedicaid usually waits until other available payers are billed.

Core COB Decision Pathway

1
Ask why the patient is being seen.Work injury? Auto accident? Ordinary visit?
2
Find the patient’s role on each plan.Subscriber usually pays before dependent.
3
Compare active, retiree, and COBRA coverage.Active employment usually comes first.
4
If plans are equal, check length of coverage.The longer active plan may become primary.
5
If Medicaid is involved, place it near the end.Medicaid is usually payer of last resort.

Now Add the Birthday Rule

The birthday rule is not all of COB. It is a special rule for a dependent child covered by both parents’ plans.

Earlier birthday in the calendar year = primary plan.
Use month and day only. The year does not matter.

Same Birthday Tie-Breaker

If both parents have the same birthday, check which parent has been covered by their plan longer.

If...Then...

Same birthdayLonger coverage wins

Custody and Step-Parent Order

Custodial parent means the parent the child primarily lives with or the parent who has legal/physical custody. Non-custodial parent means the other parent who does not have primary custody.

Separated with no court order: the child’s custodial household comes first.
1
Custodial parentThe parent the child lives with most of the time. Example: Mother if the child lives with mother.
2
Custodial parent’s spouseThe step-parent in the child’s main household. Example: Mother’s husband / step-father.
3
Non-custodial parentThe other biological/legal parent outside the child’s main household. Example: Father if the child does not live with father.
4
Non-custodial parent’s spouseThe step-parent in the other household. Example: Father’s wife / step-mother.

Who Brought the Child to the Clinic?

In front-desk, billing, and coding situations, do not assume the person who brings the child is automatically the insurance subscriber or the person legally responsible for payment.

TermMeaningBilling Warning
Accompanying adultThe adult who brings the child to the visitMay not be responsible for payment
Parent/guardianAdult with parental or legal authorityCan usually consent, depending on policy
GuarantorPerson financially responsible for the billOften listed on the patient account
SubscriberPerson who owns the insurance policyNot always the same as guarantor
PolicyholderAnother word often used for subscriberVerify plan details before billing
Important: The person who brings the child to the clinic may be an accompanying adult, but the insurance subscriber and guarantor may be different people.
Billing mindset: Ask: Who has legal authority? Who owns the insurance? Who is financially responsible?
